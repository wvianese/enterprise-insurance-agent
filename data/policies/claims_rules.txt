General Claims Rules

Claim Assessment

Claim eligibility depends on the customer's active policy, the type and cause of damage, the location of the damage, applicable exclusions, previous claims, and any relevant deductibles.

A claim should not be approved solely because the customer's policy generally covers the reported type of loss. All applicable policy conditions and exclusions must also be evaluated.

Previous Claims

Previous approved claims may affect eligibility when the customer's policy contains a claim-frequency restriction.

Only approved claims count toward an approved-claim limit unless the applicable policy explicitly states otherwise.

A denied previous claim does not count toward an approved-claim limit unless specifically stated by the policy.

Claim History

Claims history should be considered only when relevant to a rule contained in the customer's policy.

The existence of an unrelated previous claim should not automatically cause a new claim to be denied.

For example, a previous approved theft claim should not be treated as an approved flood claim when evaluating a flood-specific claim limit.

Evidence Requirements

Customers may be required to provide photographs, repair estimates, receipts, police reports, proof of ownership, or other reasonable evidence depending on the type of claim.

A lack of required evidence may prevent a final coverage determination.

Damage Location

The location of damage may affect coverage.

Policy-specific restrictions involving basements, detached structures, vehicles, commercial areas, or other locations must be checked before determining whether a loss is covered.

Deductibles

An approved claim may still require the customer to pay the applicable deductible.

A deductible does not mean that a loss is excluded. It represents the portion of an otherwise covered loss that the customer is responsible for paying.

Insufficient Information

When available customer data, claims history, or policy information is insufficient to determine coverage, the claims assistant should clearly state what additional information is required.

The claims assistant should not invent missing customer information, claim details, policy terms, or coverage decisions.

Conflicting Information

If customer records, claims history, and policy documentation appear to conflict, the claims assistant should identify the conflict rather than silently choosing one source.

Policy-specific coverage terms should be applied to the customer's active policy rather than to another insurance product.

Coverage Decisions

The claims assistant may explain whether available information suggests that a claim is likely covered, excluded, or requires additional information.

The assistant should explain the evidence and policy rules used in reaching that conclusion rather than providing an unsupported decision.
